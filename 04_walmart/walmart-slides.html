The problem

Walmart wants to predict weekly sales, and understand what drives them

“Build a machine learning model able to estimate the weekly sales in their stores, with the best precision possible on the predictions made. Such a model would help them understand better how the sales are influenced by economic indicators, and might be used to plan future marketing campaigns.”Project brief, Walmart marketing team

Two questions

  • Can we predict a store's weekly sales?
  • What explains them: the economy, or something else?

The plan, in three parts

  • Part 1: explore the data, then clean it
  • Part 2: a baseline, the linear regression. Score it, read its coefficients
  • Part 3: a regularised model, against overfitting

What the exploration shows

  • Sales vary by a factor of 7 between stores
  • No indicator strongly linked to sales: the strongest, the price index, is at −0.29
  • Price index and unemployment barely move within a store (99 % and 91 % of their variance is between stores)

How I made it usable

  • 14 rows without sales dropped: never invent the target
  • 5 outliers dropped, brief's rule mean ± 3 std: all store 12
  • Day of week dropped: every date is a Friday
  • Other holes filled in the pipeline, from training rows only

Why a scikit-learn pipeline. Filling holes and scaling are learned on the training rows only, then applied as-is to the test rows. The test never leaks into the model. One object does it all, in the right order, every time.
Why the store is a category. Store 20 is not twenty times store 1. The pipeline turns the store number into 18 yes/no columns, one per store, store 1 being the reference. And why cross-validation: 27 test rows can be lucky, so the model is scored 50 times on different splits.

Key finding
Part 2 · Read the model

Almost all the accuracy comes from which store it is

The same linear regression, trained three times. R² in cross-validation.
The coefficients agree. The largest are the stores: up to 1.4 M$ a week of gap with store 1. The largest others, price index and unemployment, are about 0.1 M$.
Why. Store averages range from 0.3 M$ to 2.2 M$ a week. Knowing the store is almost knowing its sales.
But. The price index varies 99 % between stores, 1 % within a store. This dataset cannot separate its effect from the store's.

Limits & choices
Part 3 · Regularisation

Regularisation confirms the model more than it fixes it

Close bars = little overfitting. Lasso (alpha 681, grid search) sets 1 coefficient of 26 to zero.

Known limits

  • 3 to 12 weeks per store: little history
  • No store size, no location: the file does not say why stores differ
  • Store 12 removed by the outlier rule: an unknown store is treated like store 1

Takeaways
Conclusion

Precision was there. The useful answer is where it comes from.

  • A good order of magnitude for a known store: 94 % of the variation, 11 % average error
  • But no answer on how the economy moves sales: the store identity hides it
  • To plan a campaign, Walmart needs more history per store, store size and location, and past campaigns
  • Next step: a time-based split, to measure real forecasting
