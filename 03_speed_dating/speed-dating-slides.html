The problem

Tinder gets fewer matches, and wants to know why people say yes

“They are experiencing a decrease in the number of matches, and they are trying to find a way to understand what makes people interested into each other.”Project brief, Tinder marketing team

Two questions

  • What goes with a “yes” to a second date?
  • Do people know it themselves? What they say they want, versus what they do

The deliverable: one notebook with

  • Descriptive statistics
  • Visualisations
  • Captions and interpretations linked to the second-date question
Four of the brief's five exploration ideas are answered: least desirable attributes · attractiveness, said vs done · shared interests vs same race · first or last date of the night.

Why ranks in step 3. People declared points out of 100; the decisions give a correlation. Two different scales, so I compare the order, not the numbers.
Why a confidence interval in step 4. A gap of 8.5 points could come from chance. The interval gives the range where the true gap sits, with 95 % confidence. If zero is outside, chance is unlikely. Checked with a second method that resamples participants, not rows.

Limits & choices
Notebook §8 · Date order · Limits

Being first helps. Being last, we cannot tell.

+8.5 pts
first date of the evening
49.9 % yes vs 41.4 % for the other dates · 95 % interval +4.2 to +12.8 · zero excluded
+3.4 pts
last date of the evening
95 % interval −0.9 to +7.7 · zero inside · no conclusion

Known limits

  • Marks are given after the date, by the person who decides: a link, not a cause
  • Shared interests are marked after the date; origin is known before. Not an equal comparison
  • Four-minute dates in 2002-2004, not an app

Choices I defend

  • Ranks, not raw numbers, to compare “said” and “done”
  • A second check that resamples participants, because one person's dates are not independent
  • Four evenings excluded only where their scale differs

Takeaways
Conclusion

Looks come first. Compatibility is the hidden lever.

  • Attractiveness is the first thing that goes with a yes, even when people do not say it
  • What people under-declare is compatibility: fun and shared interests rank 2nd and 3rd in decisions
  • For Tinder: show shared interests on profiles, not only photos
  • My data does not prove it will raise matches. It says what to test, on real users
